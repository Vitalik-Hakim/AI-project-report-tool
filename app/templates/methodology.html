Methodology and Model Architecture

Detailed technical description of our hybrid analysis engine, combining classical deterministic financial formulas with calibrated statistical classifiers and LLM generative briefs.

1. Machine Learning Distress Classifier

Random Forest Training and Calibration

Our predictive model is a Calibrated Random Forest Classifier trained on the american_bankruptcy.csv dataset. This historical corpus contains thousands of corporate years of US public company filings, mapping bankruptcies against healthy operating baselines.

Model Specifications:

  • Dataset: American Bankruptcy dataset (~11,000 corporate records).
  • Classifier: Random Forest ensemble calibrated via sigmoid/isotonic regression to output true probability metrics (rather than uncalibrated vote percentages).
  • Output: Calculated probability of financial insolvency/distress within the next 12-24 months.

2. Classical Rule-Based Models

Altman Z-Score (Insolvency Screening)

Developed by Edward Altman in 1968, this linear combination model evaluates five financial ratios to assess bankruptcy risk:

Z = 1.2 · X₁ + 1.4 · X₂ + 3.3 · X₃ + 0.6 · X₄ + 1.0 · X₅
X₁ = Working Capital / Total Assets — measures short-term liquidity.
X₂ = Retained Earnings / Total Assets — measures cumulative profitability.
X₃ = EBIT / Total Assets — measures productivity of assets.
X₄ = Market Value of Equity / Total Liabilities — measures financial leverage.
X₅ = Net Sales / Total Assets — measures asset turnover.

Distress Zone

< 1.81

Grey Zone

1.81 – 2.99

Safe Zone

> 2.99

Piotroski F-Score (Fundamental Trends)

Developed by Joseph Piotroski in 2000, this 9-point checklist measures operational improvement and financial stability trends across three pillars:

Profitability (4 pts)

  • Positive Net Income
  • Positive Cash Flow (CFO)
  • Improving Asset Return (ROA)
  • CFO > Net Income

Leverage / Liquidity (3 pts)

  • Decreasing Long-Term Debt
  • Improving Current Ratio
  • No New Shares Issued

Efficiency (2 pts)

  • Improving Gross Margin
  • Improving Asset Turnover

Beneish M-Score (Earnings Quality)

Developed by Messod Beneish in 1999, this model uses 8 key ratios to detect whether a company is likely manipulating its earnings:

M = -4.84 + 0.920 · DSRI + 0.528 · GMI + 0.404 · AQI + 0.892 · SGI
    + 0.115 · DEPI - 0.172 · SGAI + 4.679 · TATA - 0.327 · LVGI
DSRI: Days Sales in Receivables Index
GMI: Gross Margin Index
AQI: Asset Quality Index
SGI: Sales Growth Index
DEPI: Depreciation Index
SGAI: Sales General & Admin Index
LVGI: Leverage Index
TATA: Total Accruals to Total Assets
Risk Classification Threshold: M-Score > -1.78 indicates manipulation risk
3. Model Consensus & AI Synthesis

Consensus Logic and AI Summaries

To merge the deterministic checks with the statistical classifier, we compute a consensus score:

Rule-Based Composite Confidence

Aggregates Altman rating (up to 35%), Piotroski checklist score (up to 35%), and Beneish manipulation interpretation (up to 30%) into a weighted index.

Agreement vs. Divergence Status

Calculates the absolute difference between Rule-Based Confidence and ML Model Confidence. If the gap is ≤ 15 points, they are marked in Consensus/Agreement. If it exceeds 15 points, they are marked as Divergence, raising a flag for human review.

Mistral AI Synthesis Brief

The final text summary is compiled using the Mistral Large Latest model. Mistral is provided with the exact output array and values from the execution scripts, translating them into a balanced financial assessment covering overall status, strongest signals, and risk areas, without altering any calculation outputs.
